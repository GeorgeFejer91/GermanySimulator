# Gameplay authority

## Core loop

The player has three deliberately fictional administration days to complete eight linked procedures. Progress alternates between travelling through the city, locating the correct office, speaking to officials, completing absurd forms, collecting resources, and surviving petty rule enforcement.

Mission order:

1. Bürgeramt — permission to submit an application (`A38`).
2. Hausverwaltung — Wohnungsgeberbestätigung.
3. Bürgeramt — supplementary sheet.
4. Finanzamt — tax registration.
5. Krankenkasse — insurance evidence.
6. Amt für Stadtbild — align three nonconforming objects.
7. Ausländerbehörde — residence evidence.
8. Ausländerbehörde — fictional citizenship application.

## Controls

- Desktop: WASD or arrow keys move; Shift runs; E interacts; `§` or `Q` activates the law power after unlock.
- On the untouched title screen, pressing `S` or clicking/tapping the unchanged `S` in `SIMULATOR` is an intentionally undisclosed shortcut that starts the game after the HUM-01/DE questionnaire.
- Phone: the on-screen directional dock moves and the E action interacts.
- The game does not request motion permission and has no device-tilt or recalibration controls.

## Systems

- Running for too long, jaywalking, lingering on roads, evading an active police approach, random petty audits, and entering protected lawn can raise the wanted level. A continuous 2.6-second stay on ordinary forbidden grass or a road outside a marked zebra crossing adds a wanted star; the protected police garden responds after two seconds and remains deliberately harsher. Repeat violations escalate the wanted stars up to five and call progressively larger police responses.
- From two wanted stars onward, the first police car brings in a locally bundled recording of an authentic Polizei Essen Martinshorn. Its volume follows the nearest response car and rises modestly with the wanted tier; a separate CC0 German police-car pass-by recording punctuates close approaches without stacking on every frame. Pausing in a modal, clearing the response, or dropping below the car tier fades the loop. Missing recordings retain the existing synthesized violation siren.
- Schrebergarten and police-garden grass trigger deliberately disproportionate enforcement. The marked police-garden path is safe.
- Police pursue the player, issue localized barks, reduce energy on contact, and escort the player to the station garden. Every new response actor is staged beyond the active camera before approaching, so foot officers, cars, helicopters, and law-power reinforcements never pop into view. The first star now calls a foot officer, two stars add a fictional silver-and-blue `POLIZEI` car, each subsequent star adds another car, four stars add a black helicopter and searchlight, and five stars add a second helicopter. Patrol-car contact knocks the player away and removes energy; helicopter searchlights drain energy and deploy bounded foot reinforcements while the player remains on forbidden grass. A 5.8-second contact gate prevents a cluster of foot officers from removing several stars at once, keeping repeated deliberate trespass capable of reaching the vehicle tiers.
- Pursuit cars predict the player's smoothed movement vector, fan into separate slots ahead of that trajectory, brake at the intended roadblock, and orbit at roughly 175–250 world units when the player is still. At close range they switch back to direct contact. This makes several cars surround and cut off escape paths instead of forming one homing stack.
- Ground movement uses small circle-versus-world checks: the player, moving NPCs, foot police, and police cars cannot move deeper into buildings, solid street furniture, normable mission objects, trains, or one another. Axis-separated movement lets actors slide around corners without a heavyweight physics engine. Perimeter trains preserve their hard same-track gap and also stop before the player, pedestrians, police officers, response cars, or solid ground objects occupying their track; helicopters intentionally remain airborne and ignore ground collision.
- Zebra crossings span the full carriageway on the approaches to actual street intersections instead of overlapping in their centers. Each junction uses a paired north-and-west crossing with inward-facing pedestrian signals at both ends; one signal pole per crossing also carries its pedestrian-crossing sign. Entering on red earns nothing. A completed green crossing earns one Germanness point, while visibly waiting at red and then completing that crossing on the following green earns the crossing point plus one Ampel bonus. Crossing elsewhere provokes angry pedestrian dialogue before and during police escalation.
- The 11 satirical `REGEL DES AUGENBLICKS` cards rotate every 14 seconds during active play. Each new card reads its complete displayed body with a local negative-delivery Thorsten voice-clone recording; the fictional shorthand identifier remains visual, and the same body text is sent to German browser speech if its MP3 is unavailable. The initial card is read after the welcome dialogue, and the voice toggle applies to every reading.
- A 0–15 Germanness social-currency meter rewards concrete rule-following: collecting Pfand, finding a previously uncollected regional sausage, completing a marked zebra crossing after entering on green, waiting visibly at a red pedestrian Ampel and then completing that crossing on the following green, and correctly answering a roaming citizenship-test question. Waiting alone never awards a point, and entering on red invalidates both crossing rewards. A wrong test answer removes one point, never dropping the meter below zero. Its fill begins gray and progressively reveals saturated Schwarz-Rot-Gold; at 15/15 it is the undimmed German flag. Every actual point increase also triggers a brief black-red-gold edge flash, with a static short-lived edge treatment for reduced-motion users.
- Nine one-off regional sausage collectibles are scattered through the city: Nürnberger Rostbratwurst, Frankfurter Würstchen, Thüringer Rostbratwurst, Münchner Weißwurst, Bockwurst, Knackwurst, Mettwurst, Teewurst, and Berliner Currywurst. A first find adds a photographic badge to the 3×3 Wurstsammlerpass, announces `Extra Wurst! Unlocked …`, restores some energy, and awards one Germanness point. The announcement stays on screen briefly as a compact collection card with an attributed Creative Commons photograph and a two-sentence researched history; uncertain origin traditions are identified as legend rather than fact. Canvas and WebGL continue to render the world pickups procedurally from the same shared data.
- At 9 Germanness, the player permanently unlocks the `§` / `Q` law power and is explicitly prompted with a stylized animated 3D `§` keycap that remains until the power is first invoked. It quotes only real, current federal provisions verified against `gesetze-im-internet.de`, favoring obscure and syntactically dense material such as § 183a StGB, § 118 and § 127 OWiG, §§ 961–964 BGB, § 27 StVO, and the Lebensmittelbestrahlungsverordnung. Each activation draws from a shuffled non-repeating law deck, with the quote presented in a compact top-of-screen strip so the police diversion remains visible. Every one of the 13 exact displayed excerpts has a local negative-delivery Thorsten voice-clone recording, generated from the credited angry and disgusted CC0 references; the browser's German voice remains its missing-file fallback. The quote strip stays visible for the full recording. The power clears the player's active wanted status and redirects police toward a nearby crowd NPC. Police catch that pedestrian, escort them visibly to the station garden, and remove them into the Spiel-Knast; the power has a short cooldown. Reaching the threshold also makes the existing locally synthesized national-anthem arrangement the next immediate soundtrack cue, without overriding the player's music mute.
- Designated ordinary pedestrians periodically approach the player, lead with one of 32 region-appropriate intrusive remarks, and present a shuffled four-choice question. The remarks heavily favor mock compliments that praise the player's effort while implying that their German, accent, grammar, or integration is still inadequate. The mixed question deck contains 35 items adapted from the official BAMF `Gesamtfragenkatalog`, dated 7 May 2025, plus 10 original, conspicuously fictional Fahrschule-style questions about laminated signs, parking, tractors, cows, hedgehogs, and similarly over-specific traffic situations. In Berlin, the remark, question, and all four answers use Denglisch; on the Germany side, the entire encounter remains German-only. The card keeps the official task number visible for BAMF material and labels every driving-school entry `FIKTIVE SPIELFRAGE`. The civic pool excludes questions about rights, antisemitism, National Socialism, and historical responsibility from this joke mechanic.
- A wrong quiz answer triggers the visible-and-recorded `Nein! Nein! Nein!` sting credited in `assets/voices/LICENSES.md`, with the existing browser voice as its load-failure fallback. Both paths continue to respect the player's voice-off setting.
- Forbidden ground should visibly outweigh legal pedestrian space. Grass and landscaped ground are forbidden to walk on by default, roads and other vehicle surfaces are forbidden by default, and every traversable area must still provide a continuous but deliberately narrow sidewalk, marked crossing, or pedestrian path where walking is legal.
- The narrow legal route creates an intentional no-win social pressure system. Dense sidewalk crowds are solid to the player, automatically complain when the player enters their personal space, and pause or turn when bumped; if the player yields by stepping onto grass or a road, nearby pedestrians immediately complain about that new rule violation instead. Ambient NPCs therefore default to finding something to complain about even while the player is obeying the movement rules, without removing the player's legal route through the world.
- Twelve fictional `AMT-BAHN` trains roam two continuous rounded-rectangle tracks around the expanded 10,960 × 5,360 world. The original 9,840 × 4,240 city is shifted into the center of a 560-unit playable perimeter gutter, keeping both tracks and every seven-car consist clear of buildings on all four sides. Six trains share each loop, initially alternating direction. Every train remains one connected consist: one Kenney end car, five full-length CC BY-NC-SA Open L-Gauge n-Wagen passenger coaches, and one Kenney end car. The matching 260-unit center spacing and short flexible gangways keep the seven bodies connected while each car samples the curve independently. Long oriented body collision makes the full coach sides solid. Every consist has a distinct cruise speed and acceleration constant, frequently chooses a new bounded cruise rate, and often stops for several seconds without operational necessity. When two trains close on the same track, their motion is clamped at the hard no-overlap gap: both visibly bump, stop for 0.82 seconds, reverse direction, and accelerate away. Their physical car order remains stable, so no coach teleports, splits, passes, or flips to the other end. A player cannot walk through a coach, and a train brakes before the player, pedestrians, police, response vehicles, props, or normable objects on its path. Each new player-caused stop reserves the supplied Buxtehude recording, regardless of the normal rotation cooldown, and still triggers the short EBO gameplay warning. While the player is near either loop, the five-file station-hall pool rotates normally as distance-scaled audio-text events. An active recording plays to completion, displays no announcement textbox, never generates replacement text, and blocks later dialogue audio until it ends. Provenance and checksums live in `assets/audio/trains/PROVENANCE.md`; no claim is made that the files are Deutsche Bahn recordings or cleared for external redistribution.
- Ordinary Bratwurst, Currywurst, and Brezel restore energy. Extra-Wurst variants are unique badge collectibles. Pfand bottles increase the Pfand count.
- Forms pause world simulation and require every field before submission.
- Pressing Start first opens the two-page satirical `HUM-01/DE` humor-competence declaration, presented in the legible Fraktur-influenced `Grenze` family as an aged printout from an invented humor office. Do not use an Old English or Gothic fallback for these forms; fall back to a plain serif if the webfont is unavailable. Both sheets start reading automatically, but neither reading is a progression gate: once the required fields, deliberately awkward stamp buttons, and handwritten signature are complete, the player may submit immediately. Premature submission stops the readout and starts the fax transition plus an exact visible/spoken fine-print reprimand at once. The fax animation advances to the next sheet or game even while that reprimand continues, and the banner never intercepts input; a following sheet's reading begins after the reprimand but is itself still non-blocking. There is no audio-repeat control. A normally completed reading proceeds directly to the locally bundled user-linked fax-machine recording; a synthesized fax/printer feed remains the load-failure fallback. The paper judders downward through a visible output slot before the next sheet or game appears. The player draws each of the two signatures directly with a mouse, trackpad, stylus, or touch input; a tap alone does not count, and signature strokes are neither serialized nor persisted. German copy describes concepts in native wording instead of importing labels such as “Deadpan.” Clicking the title screen's original English button is the only gate that selects the form's English text and speech; Deutsch buttons neither select nor clear it. The title-screen joke still turns the clicked English choice into another visible `Deutsch` button, and no UI reveals or persists the preference. Finishing the declaration starts the normal Berlin welcome and gameplay; it is satire, not a real contract or legal test.
- The title-screen language selector is a deliberate joke: choosing English turns it into Deutsch, and clicking any Deutsch choice again keeps spawning more equal-width Deutsch boxes while the existing choices shrink to fit.
- Berlin uses satirical Denglisch dialogue. This is a hard world rule: Berlin lies behind the Brandmauer; after crossing out into Deutschland, every character speaks German only. UI localization may remain English, but it must never translate Deutschland character dialogue or synthesized speech into English.
- The Brandmauer firefighter is the clearly labeled satirical Friedrich Merz character. His Merz-attributed quote pool remains pure German on both sides, and synthesized delivery should prefer an available German masculine voice. Merkel and Merz quote ownership is strict: only Merz may trigger Merz lines, only Merkel may trigger Merkel lines, and their speaker labels, textboxes, voice assignments, and quote pools must never be crossed or merged.
- The HUD exposes mission, progress, energy, forms, Pfand, Germanness, the Wurstsammlerpass count, law-power state, day, current rule, wanted status, and control mode. Region changes remain gameplay events but have no persistent region card or minimap. On phone layouts the header, mission, stats, Germanness, bark, and bottom controls use compact variants; the nine badge thumbnails collapse to the Wurst count and the center playfield stays clear.
- Optional music and speech are local browser features and must remain user-toggleable. While enabled, the soundtrack preloads and attempts to play the user-supplied intro recording immediately on page entry after its first 24 seconds have been removed. Browsers that block audible autoplay retry the preloaded intro from its beginning on the first click or key press. The intro plays exactly once behind the opening forms, ducks to a lower level while the form readout is speaking, and hands off without an intentional gap to the synthesized catalog. Each later track is a weighted random choice that excludes the current track: the national anthem, Erika, Badnerlied, Württembergerlied, and other ceremonial, hymn-like, or march-like selections receive most of the probability while all 26 arrangements remain eligible. The synthesized lead and bass play one octave lower through softer triangle and sine oscillators, restrained percussion, a low-pass filter, and a lower master level for long-session comfort. Every Bundesland still has a public-domain or traditional regional melody, Baden and Württemberg are represented separately, Swabia has two additional songs, and Thüringen also has an original Kloß-themed game jingle. Do not add another third-party music recording or copy a protected modern melody without explicit user direction and recorded provenance. The local browser-generated dialogue voice is a valued part of the game; preserve it, distribute speakers across available installed German voices, and make it speak the exact authored regional line. A bounded, provenance-recorded CC0 subset of Thorsten-Voice angry and sleepy recordings is randomly mixed into exact-matching German-side pedestrian remarks and complaints. The amused `Endlich wieder Nachschub!` and disgusted `Mist, wieder nichts geschafft.` clips are first-person player reactions to gaining or losing Germanness, respectively. Treat the categorical angry and disgusted styles as negative-valence material and sleepy as low-arousal material without claiming that the source provides numeric valence/arousal annotations.
- All recorded and synthesized audio-text events are serialized: every voiced line or train announcement plays once to completion, followed by a brief 250 ms pause before the next queued event. Train announcements have priority over dialogue waiting to start, and player-obstruction Buxtehude cues have priority over ordinary proximity announcements. Spontaneous police, pedestrian, border, and ambient barks never overlap or interrupt one another. Modal dialogue still finishes before the player can advance.
- Spoken character audio and text are locked together. A bark textbox appears only when that exact queued line begins, stays visible until its speech ends, and cannot be replaced by another bark while the first line is audible. Opening a modal dialogue cancels active and queued bark speech before showing and voicing the modal's exact line. This invariant covers synthesized speech and recorded quotations; it does not apply to music or non-verbal effects.
- Runtime audio is classified as `audio-text`, `background-music`, or `sound-effect`. While any audio-text event is active, both the intro/synthesized background music and the shared sound-effect bus duck smoothly; audio text remains at full level for intelligibility.

## World identity

The world is a gray-beige bureaucratic city with civic offices, a Faxviertel, Sparkasse/Post district, Rathaus/DIN zone, eastern Ordnungsamt/Formulararchiv/Termin districts, allotment gardens, a police garden, fax infrastructure, minor props, NPC complaints, and double railway lines sealing every outer edge. The southern energy district places a visibly closed nuclear plant—silent cooling towers, sealed red barriers, and decommissioning signage—directly beside a visibly operating coal plant with an open gate, warm windows, moving conveyor, and active smokestack. A user-supplied satirical Angela Merkel sprite meanders around both plants, cycles through short sourced quotations about Fukushima and the 2011 Atomausstieg (plus “Wir schaffen das”), and says “Sie stehen hinter mir.” when the player approaches from behind. The first nearby bark uses a short attributed recording of “Wir schaffen das”; the other lines use the browser's German voice when voice is enabled. Her 6×5 atlas retains every supplied key pose and adds three generated walking transitions per direction row. The playable world is 10,960 × 5,360 units: the established 9,840 × 4,240 city remains intact at its center, surrounded by a 560-unit playable rail-clearance gutter. The **Brandmauer**, a highly visible but fully crossable wall of flames, spans the map with Berlin behind it. It changes regional speech without acting as collision geometry. The satirical Friedrich Merz character patrols the full line, alternating directional walk and pour animations while trying to extinguish it. His 8×6 normalized atlas retains every supplied key pose, adds three generated transitions per direction/action row, and keeps a transparent inset around every cell so no frame clips his head or bleeds into its neighbor. The canonical root game progressively enables the Three.js world and locally licensed Kenney/Open L-Gauge train models when available and otherwise retains the pseudo-3D Canvas renderer with procedural rolling stock. `3d.html` remains a direct renderer diagnostic. Both views preserve the same concrete-gray, repetitive-window, municipal-office visual direction rather than a colorful or cozy European look.

A fictional Bayern-Beauftragter roams the Germany-side street grid, randomly chooses connected sidewalk stops and northbound street branches, and pauses at them before moving on. When the player is nearby on the Germany side, he selects a non-repeating short excerpt from the user-supplied Bayern recording. Each excerpt's exact transcript remains visible in the bark box for the full recording, shares the global serialized speech queue, respects voice-off, and falls back to exact-text German browser speech if its local MP3 cannot load. His 8×4 directional atlas retains all five prior key poses and adds three generated walking transitions per direction. Equal square cells with transparent per-cell padding prevent Canvas or Three.js sampling from spilling into a neighboring frame.

Merkel, Merz, and the Bayern-Beauftragter each own a circular audio vicinity. Entering it starts that character's owner-locked line or recording pool; while the player remains inside, another line plays after the character's cooldown whenever the serialized speech queue is free. Leaving the wider release radius rearms immediate playback for the next entry and prevents stale lines from being queued after departure. The exact matching textbox remains visible for every recorded or synthesized line, and manual interaction remains available at the tighter interaction radius.

## Satirical voice and dialogue

The game is an affectionate satire of life in Germany, made with love for German culture and its capacity for self-irony. It exaggerates recognizable everyday interactions with people and institutions; the joke targets bureaucratic habits, social friction, and inflexible systems rather than expressing contempt for Germans.

The creative shorthand “cultural autism” means an intentionally extreme caricature of rigid, indirect communication. It is not a diagnosis or a claim about autistic people. Apply it to all character dialogue through these rules:

- A character usually avoids stating their actual concern directly. They instead announce the rule, paragraph, norm, process, quiet-hours clause, queue convention, or technicality the player has allegedly violated.
- Characters treat their own interpretation as the only possible interpretation, even when it is incomplete, contradictory, or plainly wrong. Common sense and alternative readings do not persuade them.
- The delivery stays serious, precise, and stubborn while the situation becomes increasingly specific, circular, and absurd. Characters should believe they are being helpful and correct.
- Resolution normally requires the player to produce a stamped form, printed document, fax, written confirmation, lawyer, supervisor, or other recognized authority. A sensible explanation by itself is rarely enough.
- Institutions default to fax machines, paper copies, signatures, stamps, appointments, and in-person handoffs even when a simple digital exchange would solve the problem.
- Ambient characters default to complaining at the player. On a legal sidewalk they allege obstruction, queue, spacing, or right-of-way violations; after the player moves aside onto forbidden grass or roadway, they switch to the corresponding lawn or traffic complaint. The contradiction is the joke and should not be resolved into a socially approved position.
- Berlin’s Denglisch and the German-only regional boundary remain delivery variants of this same voice; they do not replace the voice.

Strange German idioms are a recurring part of the voice. Prefer recognizable sayings such as “Hast du Tomaten auf den Augen?”, “Jetzt haben wir den Salat”, “Das ist nicht mein Bier”, “Da wird der Hund in der Pfanne verrückt”, “Ich glaub, mein Schwein pfeift”, “Das Leben ist kein Ponyhof”, “Da liegt der Hase im Pfeffer”, and “Alles hat ein Ende, nur die Wurst hat zwei.” Characters may bureaucratically over-explain, misapply, or combine them, but the saying should connect to the current complaint, mission, or alleged rule rather than appear at random.

Regional delivery follows the player’s in-game side of the marked boundary:

- Behind the Brandmauer on the Berlin side, dialogue uses deliberately awkward Denglisch. German sayings may be partially translated or spliced into English bureaucracy, for example: “Hast du tomatoes on den Augen? The Gehwegordnung is very clearly marked.”
- On the Germany side outside the Brandmauer, dialogue is fully German, for example: “Hast du Tomaten auf den Augen? Die Gehwegordnung ist eindeutig ausgeschildert.”
- The interface can be English, but character text and browser-generated speech still follow the region rule. Deutschland dialogue must not be translated into English; Berlin dialogue must retain its authored Denglisch mixture.
- Use the game’s current region state, not assumptions about real Berlin geography, to choose the language variant. The underlying joke, rule fixation, and character intent should remain equivalent on both sides.

Dialogue should vary the alleged rule and bureaucratic mechanism so the joke does not collapse into one repeated catchphrase. The escalation may be surreal, but it must remain clearly fictional and internally consistent with the current mission.

## Satire boundary

All deadlines, rules, procedures, penalties, immigration mechanics, and enforcement are fictional game satire. The exaggerated communication style is a comic lens, not a factual claim that every German person or institution behaves this way. Political figures and posters are stylized satirical props and must not be used to imply unsupported factual claims or political endorsements.
