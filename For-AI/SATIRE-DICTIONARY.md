# German pedestrian satire dictionary

## Purpose

This is the durable writing and visual brief for the two accepted ordinary pedestrians. It is a selective comedy taxonomy, not a claim to describe every German person or a demographic model of Germany. Both characters are grumpy middle-class towel reservers: irritated before anything has happened and absolutely certain a folded piece of fabric establishes jurisdiction over public space. The crowd remains contemporary, urban and multicultural because the recurring joke is how many different people can become equally committed to this tiny procedure.

The appearance is not the archetype. Names, skin tones, family histories, ages and body types may rotate independently from the comic behavior. Never make ethnicity, nationality, accent, religion, disability, gender identity, refugee status or poverty the defect being mocked. Aim the joke at status performance, consumer ritual, rule policing, administrative certainty and the conversion of private preferences into public procedure.

## Runtime set

| Runtime id | In-world type | Comic target | Stable visual read | Procedural behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `towel-man` | LIEGENRESERVIERER | Treating a beach towel as land registry and contract law | Sun hat, shorts, white socks in sandals, tightly rolled blue-yellow towel | Claims a place by “textile Willenserklärung” and places the towel in a Vorverfahren |
| `towel-woman` | HANDTUCHHOHEIT | Queue authority and the metaphysics of reserved loungers | Sun visor, coral windbreaker, white socks in sandals, folded red-white towel | Declares that a drying reservation remains legally present and regulates the player's shadow |

The production order alternates these two accepted figures. Personal names and appearance still rotate independently, so “middle class” describes the performed behavior and props, never an ethnicity.

Both walk horizontal and vertical sidewalk routes and share one animation contract: eight authored contact/down/passing/up keys for both legs in side, front/down and back/up rows; three motion-compensated in-betweens per interval and loop seam; 32 runtime frames in each direction; a registered center and baseline; and horizontal mirroring only for left travel. They are pass-through crowd actors, so they cannot trap the player or jam each other. Runtime speech is circular-proximity based and repeats from the archetype's no-repeat regional pool. Berlin uses Denglisch; Deutschland uses German only.

## Expansion checklist

Before adding another pedestrian type:

1. Name the behavior or institution being exaggerated in one sentence. If the answer is a protected identity, stop.
2. Give the character one legible prop and one legible silhouette; do not pile on national costumes.
3. Write paired Berlin-Denglisch and German-only barks that begin with an alleged rule, process or jurisdiction problem.
4. Rotate personal names independently from the archetype and maintain a visibly mixed crowd across the complete set.
5. Author or generate the complete eight-key gait before interpolation. Passing frames must show the legs crossing, while head, torso, scale and foot baseline remain registered.
6. Run the checksum-bound critical animation gate and real-game desktop/mobile playtest before shipping. A changed sprite is unapproved until every anatomical and loop item is visually signed again.
