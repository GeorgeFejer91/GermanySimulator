# Speech catalog and subtitle authority

[`AUDIO-TEXT-LIBRARY.js`](./AUDIO-TEXT-LIBRARY.js) loads before `game.js`. Its `voices` registry assigns stable speaker IDs and stores local Voice Cloner bindings. `speechFamilies` maps browser-speech and recorded trigger families to these IDs. For Merz, Merkel, Söder/Bayern, and Alice, `profileVoiceId` points to a saved, independently voiced character profile while `voiceId` identifies current delivery. `clips` indexes every shipped foreground speech asset by stable clip ID. Each clip keeps its German spoken source, English subtitle, current recording voice ID, trigger, and file path together. `targetVoiceId` marks the separate saved profile intended to replace a legacy recording; it does not claim that the current file was generated with that profile. The five long station clips also have timed subtitle cues. The path-keyed `recordings` view still serves the subtitle broker.

As of 2026-09-30 the catalog covers **55 shipped speech clips**: six Thorsten source clips, one quiz sting, two Merkel excerpts, eight Bayern excerpts, five Alice excerpts, five train announcements, 13 law readings, 11 rule readings, and four traffic-driver shouts. The law and rule entries are generated from ordered German/English decks in this file; `game.js` uses `speechClip(id)` for exact text and paths. Original recordings and derivatives retain provenance in `assets/voices/LICENSES.md`, and train provenance remains in `assets/audio/trains/PROVENANCE.md`.

Browser-generated dialogue remains in `game.js`: source-language pools, regional variants, dynamic lines, and trigger logic. This catalog's `pools`, `lines`, `quizContexts`, and `questions` provide English subtitles; `speechFamilies` records each family's delivery and any intended character profile. The subtitle toggle stays off by default. The broker starts subtitles with actual audio playback and clears them on completion or cancellation. Textboxes remain locked to the exact spoken string. Browser speech prefers installed local voices, retries an unavailable selected voice once with the browser default, and avoids cancelling an idle engine before a new line. Visible English subtitles occupy a fixed two-line strip at the bottom edge on desktop and mobile; phone controls and dialogue rise by its rendered height. Pretext measures each English caption against that strip and splits long translations into fitting parts. Authored train cue windows retain their start and end times; parts within each window advance by proportional text length against the Web Audio clock. Other recordings use their decoded audio duration with the same clock. Browser speech advances parts from utterance word-boundary positions, with a duration estimate if boundaries stop arriving. The toggle stays above the strip, and an impossible single-grapheme fit can scroll within it.

Official document readings and the opening mission briefing retain the existing clunky browser-generated German speech. The `narrator` catalog role has no Voice Cloner profile and should not be rendered into a separate narrator clip set.

The four general train clips belong to station-platform proximity. The Buxtehude clip belongs to rail-track presence and new player-caused train stops. These triggers change no transcript, subtitle cue, recording, voice owner, or playback-level rule.

The four traffic-driver lines are original short reactions informed by [German road-rage vocabulary](https://www.reddit.com/r/German/comments/129uvu5/road_rage_vocab/) and [reported street confrontations](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskAGerman/comments/1f1lfs3/a_man_got_angry_at_us_for_crossing_the_street_at/). They are not quotations attributed to Reddit users. The car that first brakes for the player selects a Berlin-Denglisch or Deutschland-German clip; the immediate horn is a separate nonverbal effect. A missing MP3 falls back to browser speech of the same displayed line.

For a new character, follow [`VOICE-SYNTH-PROTOCOL.md`](./VOICE-SYNTH-PROTOCOL.md): create one reusable Voice Cloner profile, add its voice ID, then add a clip ID per approved line. Reference that clip ID from the owning game trigger. Background music and nonverbal effects are excluded.

The quiz card's main paragraph contains the full spoken passerby remark, conversational context, and question. Browser-speech dialogue appears on the utterance start event; recorded lines reveal their text in the same task as source start. Rule and subtitle-approval readings use the shared bark box, and each HUM-01 spoken chunk appears in its readout status while it plays. Browser speech prefers installed local voices, retries an unavailable selected voice once with the browser default, and avoids cancelling an idle engine before a new line. A stalled browser voice releases a readable line and the queue after its startup watchdog.

The four original `KL` Kugellager questions share the quiz speech path. Their Schweinfurt/Sweden context and English question subtitles are keyed by source ID in the runtime catalog; German-only and Berlin-Denglisch prompts remain authored in `game.js`.

The caption's two-line bottom slot remains measurable when its text is hidden. The dock, Germanness rail, subtitle toggle, footer, dialogue, and reading modals use that reserved height whether a caption is empty, short, or long. Dialogue keeps a fixed outer box; long source text scrolls inside it while its action buttons stay in place. Caption or textbox changes therefore do not move the surrounding controls. Viewport resizing updates the shared slot and repaginates active captions.
