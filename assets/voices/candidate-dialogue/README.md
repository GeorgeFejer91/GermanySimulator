# Opt-in candidate dialogue preview

These 223 MP3s are selected **review candidates**, generated with Chatterbox Multilingual V2 0.1.7 (model revision `5bb1f6ee58e50c3b8d408bc82a6d3740c2db6e18`, MIT model terms) from hash-verified CC0 EmoDB references. This includes 114 fixed-character/Bürgeramt lines, twelve city police barks and four Bürgeramt phone lines for Heinrich Wachtmeister, all 48 barks owned by twelve separately named moving crowd actors, and all 45 inner-monologue lines for Hans-Peter Mustermann. Each entry in `manifest.json` records the exact line, named `voiceId`, saved Secret Tunnel profile UUID, reference hash, output hash, and independent ASR result. The original EmoDB audio is not included here. Its per-character source filenames, speaker IDs, labels, and hashes are in `For-AI/VOICE-CAST.json`.

Open the game with `?voicePreview=1` and enable voices to audition candidates in city NPC dialogue, Bürgeramt character/clerk scenes, police barks, named moving towel-pedestrian barks, and player inner speech. The Bürgeramt QR invitation carries this preview flag to the phone, where Heinrich's four exact phone lines use the same named profile and timing receipts. Normal gameplay keeps the existing speech path and browser voice choices. A candidate is chosen only for an exact `(voiceId, text)` match; a missing or failed recording falls back to browser speech. Every currently authored fixed-character, clerk, police, named-crowd bark, and player inner-monologue line has an opt-in candidate; several selected takes still have changed or ambiguous words under independent transcription and need listening or retakes. Speech remains tied to the visible dialogue and is cancelled when the cue changes.

Independent German ASR matched 158 of 223 selected lines word-for-word after case and punctuation normalization. Seven player takes were assembled from language-aware German and English fragments, with exact fragment checks or explicit `Leergut` compound-spacing equivalence; four of these also pass whole-line German ASR. Four crowd takes use the spoken equivalent of written `07:04`; their words match after explicit clock-time and compound-spacing normalization. Eight further crowd takes combine an independently recognized English word with a same-speaker German fragment; their first-word cut and join are documented in the manifest. These checks are recorded separately from literal whole-line ASR agreement. Brunhilde Knick's `A38` line now uses a documented same-profile splice: a retake of the form-number sentences plus the original take's exact final question. Independent ASR matched all intended words on the encoded MP3; the join and performance still need listening. Dieter Papiermann's `nennt das` line now joins its original first and last sentences to a same-profile exact middle-sentence retake; the encoded MP3 passed independent word-exact ASR, while its joins and demeanor still need listening. The 65 whole-line mismatches still need listening and possible retakes, especially mixed German/English words and written clock times. **None of these clips is release-cleared merely because it appears in this preview.** Listen to all 223 for exact words, identity, emotion, joins, artifacts and reference echo before enabling any clip by default. Source emotion labels are selection evidence, not proof that generated output retains validated valence/arousal.

A read-only FFmpeg loudness pass on 2026-10-08 decoded all 223 MP3s without errors. The median was -20.11 LUFS; 219 clips exceed the eventual -1.5 dBTP ceiling and 209 fall outside the eventual -18 ±0.3 LUFS target before normalization. Normalize each accepted clip after the listening decision and update its hash in the shipped speech catalogue; do not treat level normalization as evidence that an unreviewed voice performance is suitable.
