# Neighbor level character profiles

These profiles own the voices of the four JWD neighborhood residents. Shared
mission and tour behavior remains in [JWD gameplay](../../../For-AI/GAMEPLAY.md#jwd-home-and-neighbors).
Heiko's characterization below was approved by George on 2026-10-14 and takes
precedence over generic rule-reciting dialogue defaults and older Heiko lines.

## Heiko Maaslos

The selected voice is **GS Heiko Maaslos (20s reference)**, restored at George’s explicit request on 2026-10-18. The fixed binding and accepted/held recording coverage are owned by [VOICE-CAST.json](../../../For-AI/VOICE-CAST.json) and the [voice protocol](../../../For-AI/VOICE-SYNTH-PROTOCOL.md#heiko-professor-reference-restored--2026-10-18).

Professor of systems engineering (Systemtechnik), Inga's husband and Hartmut's
father. Intelligent, insecure, highly neurotic and arrogant, but not articulate.
He needs control and reassurance while preserving the appearance that everyone
is friendly and getting along. He expects deference without admitting that he
is making a demand.

His central mechanism is **imposing something while framing it as a favor to
the listener**. He makes his private preference a Selbstverständlichkeit:
something any reasonable neighbor would already understand and do. His unwanted
reminder supposedly saves the listener trouble or helps them fit in. Compliance
is assumed, and refusal becomes the listener's failure to preserve the pleasant
relationship. The authoring shorthand is German **Kleinmut**: petty anxiety and
a lack of courage to own a demand directly, expressed through this fictional
character's behavior.

Write him with these habits:

- Start with a small friendly approach: "Ach, schön, dass Sie da sind" or
  "Ich wollte nur kurz …". He wants to seem familiar and harmless.
- Slip the instruction into assumed agreement: "Das machen Sie dann am
  Wochenende, ja?" or "Ist ja selbstverständlich …". He is not asking an open
  question.
- Present the intervention as help for the listener: "Ich sag's Ihnen lieber
  gleich, dann haben Sie hinterher keinen Ärger." He claims the favor while
  assigning the work.
- Invoke reciprocity selectively: "Ich achte bei uns auch drauf." His own
  standards supposedly entitle him to manage someone else's choices.
- Use hesitations, restarts, incomplete clauses and "also", "ja", "nur",
  "eigentlich". His intelligence appears in the assumptions and observations;
  his speech stays awkward and ordinary rather than eloquent.
- His insistence uses **Floskeln** that presume agreement: "Sie wissen ja, wie
  das ist", "Sie wissen ja, wie es ist", "Man kennt das ja", "Das versteht sich
  doch von selbst" and "Unter Nachbarn macht man das ja so". George emphasized
  this on 2026-10-15. They turn his preference into an allegedly shared norm;
  vary them with the situation instead of making one a repeated catchphrase.
- When resisted, become wounded and faintly threatening through social pressure:
  having to repeat himself would be "auch für mich unangenehm". He quickly
  restores the fiction of agreement: "Aber das müssen wir ja gar nicht."

He does **not** directly quote laws or paragraph numbers at the player, deliver
a polished legal lecture, openly announce his manipulation, or land a neat
aphorism in every sentence. Verified rules may inform the situation without
becoming his spoken script. Engineering vocabulary and regional Denglisch may
appear sparingly when natural; they must not displace his insecure, halting
neighbor voice. The approved sample uses "Sie"; friendliness does not require
switching to "du".

George reaffirmed on 2026-10-15 that this style applies to **all** Heiko speech:
introductions, repeat and follow invitations, guided stops, travel lines,
fallback complaints and remarks to Hartmut. The hedge example is the reference
for his whole voice, not a special case confined to one conversation.

### Approved voice sample

> „Ach, schön, dass Sie da sind. Ich wollte nur kurz … die Hecke da, die kommt jetzt schon ziemlich zu uns rüber. Das machen Sie dann am Wochenende, ja? Also, ein bisschen zurück. Ist ja selbstverständlich, dass man da gegenseitig … also, ich achte bei uns auch drauf.
>
> Wir wollen hier ja entspannt miteinander sein. Ich bin wirklich der Letzte, der wegen so was … Nur wenn ich’s erst dreimal sagen muss, dann ist das natürlich auch für mich unangenehm. Aber das müssen wir ja gar nicht. Sie sehen’s ja jetzt auch.“

George approved this sample as in character. Its social imposition, presumed
agreement, hesitant delivery and threatened loss of friendliness are the voice
reference. Favor framing should also be explicit where the situation supports it.

## Inga Maaslos

Heiko's wife, Hartmut's mother; works in fictional People & Culture. Bright,
performatively welcoming and status-conscious. She recasts imposed chores as
positive opportunities the newcomer supposedly chose. Privacy becomes a lack
of community openness; unsolicited allocations become inclusion and engagement.

Her dialogue centers on unsolicited coaching about how the player should behave
as a good neighbor: greet promptly, remain available, keep up appearances and
volunteer before being asked. Use fluent, upbeat HR language, superficial
Denglisch and presumed enthusiasm: Cultural Fit, Ownership, Touchpoints,
Commitment and Action Points. Each pleasant suggestion quietly becomes an
expectation. She believes she is supporting the newcomer; do not have her
explain her manipulation or abandon the friendly tone for an overt threat.

Unlike Heiko's halting reassurance, her pressure comes through polished positive
framing. Her electric car and selective family anecdotes serve her public
self-image. She boasts about organizing Hartmut's activities, supporting the
family and still finding time for the community. Her supposed selflessness sets
the standard for the player's contribution; family stories support this voice
rather than taking over the conversation. Keep a few concrete family examples,
including the dinosaur day, and let her turn her workload into another cheerful
request for help. This direction was requested by George on 2026-10-21.

## Frau Schwabingen

A house-proud Swabian Kehrwoche enthusiast. Cleanliness, clipped hedges, sweeping
and a private rota are measures of neighborly worth. She treats her own diligent
work as grounds to supervise everyone else's free time.

Her voice is practical, stern and personally invested, with deliberately exaggerated Swabian forms
such as "i", "isch" and "net". Friendship is conditional on visible upkeep.
She can acknowledge that her rota is private while treating it as morally
binding. Preserve the distinction between her personal standards and actual
rules; her imposition is rooted in house pride rather than Heiko's need to seem
inoffensive or Inga's corporate welcome.

## Hartmut Maaslos

Renamed from Theodore by George on 2026-10-14. Use Hartmut Maaslos in all new
dialogue and character context. Existing technical identifiers such as
`jwd-theodore`, `jwd-theodore-maaslos` and `theodore-*` clip/asset names retain
their stable bindings; they identify this same character and are not names to
use in dialogue. His selected saved profile is now `GS Hartmut Maaslos (CC0
child character)`; the earlier `GS Theodore Maaslos` adult-actor profile is
preserved. The shared voice cast owns UUIDs, reference hashes and source records.

Heiko and Inga's eight-year-old son. Speaks six languages; his parents worry about
his Latin milestones. He wants time to play rather than another achievement,
schedule or learning objective. He has his own voice, choices and play route.

Speak plainly and concretely, with a child's imagination and quick observations
of adult contradictions. Paving stones are islands, the joints are lava, and the
fallen garden gnome is a referee. Humor grows from what the adults actually do;
he is not a miniature professor delivering adult satire. He invites the player
to play and can lead his father, reversing the usual supervision. Keep his
speech distinct from both parents' status anxiety.

## Ede · Einbruch mit Anspruch

Archived on 2026-10-18 at George’s request. Retained as recovery/voice context only; see the [archived encounter](../archive/ede.md). He is not in the active neighbor cast.

A former recurring visitor to the neighbor level, with actor ID `jwd-burglar` and stable
voice ID `jwd-ede-einbruch`. The shared voice cast owns his selected profile and
recording bindings.

A burglar with standards who finds the player's sparse household disappointing.
He complains about "negative loot" and suggests the Maaslos house as a more
promising destination; the joke is that robbing the player would mean leaving
furniture behind. His delivery is dry, low-energy and sardonically resigned,
rather than aggressive or menacing.

Use ordinary colloquial German with Berlin forms such as "Dit" and "wa".
He says what he wants directly, unlike Heiko's hesitant favors or Inga's upbeat
allocations. Keep the humor concrete and tied to the household he actually sees.
His encounter uses the existing visit and dialogue guards; a character profile
does not add a new burglary mission or reward.
